# Emphasis, intensity and contrast

## Emphasis is selective

Loud or important sounds get emphasis, most often by larger spacing before them. But emphasis
only works by contrast: as one forum guide puts it, "If everything is emphasized, then nothing
is" (Xelasto). Decide at the level of the **phrase** (typically four bars): within a phrase some
strong sounds are deliberately played down so the truly important ones stand out. Importance comes
not only from loudness but from structure, lyrics, a distinctive melody or rhythm.

## Ways to express intensity

- **Density**: more objects (more 1/2, more 1/4) in intense sections is the most common tool.
- **Spacing**: wider jumps for intense parts; **anti-jumps** (sudden close spacing after wide) and
  **accelerated spacing** (distance growing across a passage) create build-up and release.
- **Movement**: comfortable flow in calm parts, sharper or more awkward movement where the music
  is aggressive.
- **Drain sections**: deliberately lighter passages that match quiet music and give the player
  room.
- **Kiai** for the strongest section.

## Visual spacing and consistency

Mappers judge spacing by what the eye sees (equal visual distance), not by a distance-snap
number, and keep it consistent: small random differences read as mistakes. Similar musical
sounds get similar treatment, except where phrase-level contrast calls for a change.
