# Rhythm: what to map

## Layers

A song is made of layers: percussion (kick, snare, hats), the melody instruments, the voice, the
bass. A map mostly follows one layer at a time and switches between them on purpose, for emphasis
and contrast. Following a layer consistently is what makes a map readable: the player learns what
the clicks mean.

- Common practice: follow the **voice or melody in calm parts and the drums in intense parts**;
  most active objects (circles, slider heads) should land on the layer being followed, with
  occasional sounds from others, as long as the main layer stays obvious.
- In songs with vocals the voice is usually the most prominent thing, so following it closely
  represents the song well. Without vocals, the drums and the rhythm section carry the map.
- Map what is loudest and most prominent as **active** (clicked) and quieter, held or subtle
  sounds as **passive** (slider bodies, tails, nothing).

## Overmapping

Placing objects where nothing in the music asks for them creates a layer that is not in the song.
It can make a map more interesting, but done inconsistently it hides what the mapper follows.
Streams in particular should exist only where an instrument plays them.

## Filler rhythm

Not every sound needs emphasis: unemphasised passages can be mapped with simpler, regular rhythm
(pishifat's "Filler Rhythm" episode), keeping attention for what matters.
