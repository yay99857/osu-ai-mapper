# Flow and patterns

## Flow

Flow is how naturally the cursor moves through a map. Smooth, circular or semicircular paths
(clockwise or anticlockwise, alternating with neutral transitions) feel comfortable; abrupt pace or
direction changes feel awkward. Movement that repeats familiar paths (back-and-forth jumps, slider
pairs) is easy because the player predicts it, and repetition mirrors repeated music. Sliders can be
bent to support the intended direction. Comfortable flow is not automatically good: awkward
movement is a legitimate tool for aggressive music, and variety matters.

**Angles, careful with the convention.** Mappers talk about the *interior* angle at an object:
a "sharp" angle (interior well under 90°, the cursor snaps back) is snappy and comfortable for
jumps; around 90° interior is the uncomfortable zone; wide angles (near 180° interior, almost
straight) are smooth. Our studies measure the *turn* (change of direction), where turn = 180° -
interior: human 1/2 jumps turn 135-165° most often (stars, sharp interior angles), 75-105° (square,
the awkward zone) only about 10% of the time — consistent with the advice.

## Jump patterns

Back-and-forth (between two spots), triangles, squares, stars, zigzags, wide "oibon" jumps across
the screen, slider-based jumps (back-and-forth or triangles anchored on sliders, endpoint slider
jumps, orbital sliders), anti-jumps.

## Formations

Geometric shapes (triangles, squares, diamonds, polygons, circles), symmetry (mirror or rotation,
often 30/60/120°), slider blankets (circles wrapped by a slider's curve), parallel sliders, flowers
(circles around a centre), tornado combos, honeycomb grids, zig-zags. Purpose: guide movement,
make patterns clean and recognisable, vary what skill a section tests. A new combo is the natural
place to start a new pattern.

## Visual consistency

Similar sliders bent the same way within a section, straight sliders tilted consistently, equal
visual spacing inside a pattern: clean structure reads as intention.
